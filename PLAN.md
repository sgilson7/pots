# Plan

Stage 2, `B2 Require step-by-step justification`. Approved in advance by the brief's Autonomy paragraph; decisions the plan did not fix go into `SECOND-ORDER.md` as they are made.

Each decision is listed in build order with its reason and the test that will show it holds.

## Round 1: the match, arcade, online, publish

### 1. Units and time

- **Decision.** Fixed tick of 30 per second. Positions are `i32` in sixteenths of a Warcraft III map unit (`SUB = 16`); squared distances are `i64`. Angles use `fx.rs` turns (65 536 to a circle). Every speed, range and radius in data is in map units, as DotA's sources give them, so the literature review's numbers go into data unchanged.
- **Reason.** D2 bans floats. Sixteenths keep a 300-speed hero's step (10 units a tick) exact, and the map (about 16 000 units across) fits in `i32` with room to spare.
- **Test.** A hero ordered to walk 3 000 units at speed 300 arrives in 300 ticks (worked by hand); the same seed and orders give the same checksum twice.

### 2. Rules and state are separate

- **Decision.** `Rules` (heroes, abilities, items, creeps, map) is parsed once from `data/*.json`. `World` holds only what changes, and derives `Clone`.
- **Reason.** Rollback clones the world every confirmed tick; the rules must not be cloned with it.
- **Test.** Cloning a world at minute ten takes under 100 µs (measured in `MEASUREMENTS.md`).

### 3. The map

- **Decision.** `data/map.json` gives lanes as polylines, the river, high-ground regions, tower, barracks, Sphere, shop, fountain, rune, camp and boss positions, after DotA's layout. Trees are generated by the core from a seed: every pathing cell (64 units) that is not cleared by a lane, path, base or clearing gets a tree at the generator's density. Trees can be cut (by items and abilities) and regrow after the DotA time.
- **Reason.** Hand-placing thousands of trees is not possible here; a seeded generator gives the same forest to every player.
- **Test.** Every lane polyline is walkable from fountain to fountain on the generated map; every camp and rune is reachable.

### 4. Movement and pathing

- **Decision.** A* on the 64-unit grid for heroes and neutrals, with collision circles between units; creeps follow lane waypoints and steer around each other.
- **Reason.** DotA's juking in trees depends on trees blocking paths; creep lanes do not need search.
- **Test.** A path around a tree line goes around it; a creep wave reaches the first enemy tower.

### 5. Combat

- **Decision.** DotA's model: base attack time over (1 + attack speed), attack point and backswing, projectile or instant, damage with the attack-type and armor-type table, armor reduction `0.06·a / (1 + 0.06·a)` in per-mille integers, magic resistance, pure damage, last hits and denies with DotA's rules, creep and tower aggro.
- **Reason.** "play as faithfully as possible in terms of how the game is played".
- **Test.** Hand-worked cases: 10 armor reduces 100 physical damage to 63 (37.5 % reduction rounded); a deny is refused above half health; a tower switches to a hero who attacks an allied hero in its range.

### 6. Heroes, abilities, items

- **Decision.** Ten heroes after ten DotA heroes chosen in the literature review, original names and looks. Abilities are coded in core (`abilities.rs`), numbers in `data/heroes.json`. Items and recipes in `data/items.json`, with actives in core.
- **Test.** One test per ability with a hand-worked number at one level (damage, duration, cooldown); every ability and item has a hover description in data.

### 7. Creeps, neutrals, boss, runes, economy

- **Decision.** Waves every 30 s, DotA's composition and upgrades; neutral camps every minute; the boss with its drop; runes every two minutes; DotA's gold, experience, respawn and buyback formulas.
- **Test.** Hand-worked bounty, experience split and respawn time cases from the review.

### 8. Behavior trees

- **Decision.** A behavior-tree engine in core (after Vagrancy's), trees in `data/trees/*.json`. Difficulty is a separate table of numbers (reaction ticks, last-hit skill, retreat threshold, ability use) passed to the same trees, so ally and enemy difficulty are set apart.
- **Test.** A tree that retreats below its health threshold does so; an easy bot last-hits fewer creeps than a hard bot in the same seeded lane.

### 9. Scene and HUD from the core

- **Decision.** The core builds a list of model instances (model, position, height, facing, team, animation, scale) and the camera; the page uploads models once and draws instances with WebGL 2. Models are built from primitives in `data/models.json`; the core expands them to integer vertices. The HUD, shop and menus are the core's view model, rendered by the page.
- **Test.** Every unit kind in data has a model; the scene list for a known world is the hand-counted length.

### 10. Arcade

- **Decision.** `data/arcade.json` lists missions, each with ally count (0 to 4), enemy count (1 to 5), ally and enemy difficulty, and the lesson. A free setup screen picks those four numbers directly.
- **Test.** Each mission's counts are within the brief's ranges; a full match between bots on fast-forward ends with a Sphere destroyed.

### 11. Online

- **Decision.** Trystero (Nostr) introduces browsers by room code, as in Galactic Survivors; every browser connects to every other (mesh). Rollback over orders: every browser runs the match, guesses a late order as "no new order", and rolls back when the real one arrives. Mid-match rejoin: the returning player keeps a seat token; any peer sends the last confirmed world and the orders since, while the seat runs a computer player in the meantime.
- **Test.** Core tests on a lagged, lossy wire end in agreement; a seat that leaves and returns finishes in agreement; five headless browsers play one match (the five-runner test).

### 12. Publish

- **Decision.** `sgilson7/pots` on GitHub Pages; a tile on sgilson7.github.io.
- **Test.** `./scripts/check.sh` passes; the browser check passes on the live address.
