Protection of the Spheres

A browser game that plays like the Warcraft III map Defense of the Ancients, with heroes of its own. One tab for each round of work. New rounds are added as new tabs.

Entry 1 · 15 October 2026 · brief and plan

What was asked, and the order of work

The project is a copy of Builder's template in the OGDota folder. The repository will be sgilson7/pots, served at sgilson7.github.io/pots. The plan is approved in advance, so this entry records what I will do. It does not ask permission.

Your words

The description, as given

my friend wants a game that plays as similarly as possible to the original mod for warcraft3, defense of the ancients … it should support 5v5 online multiplayer in the browser … they should be distinct from the warcraft champs but with the same abilities … a basic arcade missio mode … 0-4 ai teammates vs 1-5 ai enemies … the difficulty of the AI controlled independently, all done via behavior trees like vagrancy … rollback netcode, and allowing people to reconnect mid-game … build out the mvp for the game with 10 characters …

The full text is in BRIEF.md, word for word.

How Builder carries it

Where each part lives

LayerFor this game
Core (Rust)The whole match, at a fixed tick in integer maths with a seeded random source: movement and pathing around trees, attacks and the attack-type table, armor, abilities, items and recipes, creep waves, neutral camps, the boss, runes, gold and experience, death and respawn, vision and fog, and every computer player's behavior tree. It also builds the camera, the 3D scene list and the HUD description. Same seed and same inputs give the same match on every machine, which is what rollback needs.
Shim (wasm)Passes orders in and the scene out. Decides nothing.
PageWebGL draws the scene the core lists. The HUD, shop and menus are built from the core's view model. Mouse and keyboard become orders.
OnlineBrowsers connect to each other directly and exchange orders, tick by tick. Each browser runs the match itself, guesses what late orders were, and rolls back and replays when the real order arrives. A rejoining browser receives the match state and the orders since.
DataHeroes, abilities, items, creeps, the map layout, missions, behavior trees and every string, in JSON. Item and ability icons drawn in TikZ.
Decided without asking

Choices the brief left open

Names

Every hero, item, creep, building and the boss has an original name and look. Abilities keep their behaviour and numbers. The two sides are the Sunward and the Moonward, each guarding a Sphere. You read the names before they are final.

The DotA version

The game follows the 6.6x versions (2009 to 2011), the era most sources describe and most players remember. Where sources disagree, the review says which version a number comes from.

Online without a server

GitHub Pages serves files only, so browsers find each other through a public signaling service and then talk directly. That service is the one request to another site, and it loads only when a player opens online play.

Computer players online

Empty seats in an online room are filled by computer players. They run inside the match itself, so every browser computes the same choices.

Kept by the coding agent. The project's notebook (SECOND-ORDER.md) records each decision in more detail, numbered, as it is made.

How DotA Allstars actually worked

IceFrog's own site, read through the Wayback Machine. Its mechanics pages were captured in December 2011, which is map version 6.72/6.73: attributes, attack speed, the damage table, gold, experience, every unit's stats, runes, vision.

6.48 to 6.79c, mirrored on playdota. Used to tag every number with the version it holds for, and to catch where later versions changed it.

Never simplify

What players single out

Kept exactly

  • Gold only from the last hit; denies below half health
  • Per-hero attack point and backswing, cancelled by any order
  • The damage table, the armor formula, the hero spell resistance
  • Starting gold 603, 200 + 5 per level for a kill plus streaks, assist gold by heroes near, death costs 30 per level of unreliable gold, buyback and its cooldown, respawn 4 seconds per level
  • Waves every 30 seconds, upgrades every 7½ minutes, mega creeps after barracks fall
  • Neutrals refill on the minute if the camp is empty, so stacking and pulling work
  • Trees block paths and sight; higher ground hides from lower
  • Towers fall in order; backdoor protection; the glyph

Simplified for now

  • One courier for each side from the start, rather than bought
  • No items on the ground: a full inventory refuses a purchase
  • Backdoor protection without the "unregenerable" part
  • No Aghanim's upgrades, no Bottle-stored runes, no Cheese
  • Collision sizes and some turn rates are unverified in every source; Dota 2's values stand in
Open questions the review could not settle.

Lane creep armor (playdota prints Unarmored, the Dota 2 port says Medium; ranged-creep damage differs 2×), periodic gold (one every 0.875 or 0.8 seconds), creep aggro radius, and the map's real size. Each needs the 6.83 map file opened in the World Editor.

Sources and every number: notes/LITERATURE.md. Player quotes: notes/FEEL.md. What the data uses where the review is unverified: notes/DATA-TRANSCRIPTION.md.

Computer players

Behavior trees, and what measuring them found

Each computer player runs one tree from data/ai.json: a priority list of rules (go home when low, use a potion, buy the build's next item, retreat from a tower, cast on an enemy hero, finish a weak one, take a rune, help the creeps siege, harass, last-hit). Skill is a separate table, so the same tree plays at five levels: how often it decides, how well it times last hits, how often it denies and casts, when it retreats.

Entry 5 · 16 October 2026 · online mode

Ten browsers, rollback, and coming back

A host opens a room and shares its code or link; up to nine others join. Empty seats are played by the computer. Browsers meet through public Nostr relays and then talk to each other directly.

How it works

Rollback over orders

Every browser runs the match

The rules are deterministic: the same seed and orders give the same match bit for bit. Only orders cross the network, never the world.

Guess, then correct

A browser's own orders act two ticks later. Another seat's late order is guessed as "nothing new", which is right on most ticks; when it arrives, the world is restored to before that tick and run again.

Coming back

The address keeps a seat token. When a player drops, the computer takes the seat in every browser. On return the host sends every order so far; the browser replays the match at speed and takes the seat back.

What it cannot do yet.

If the host's browser leaves, the match ends. Two players both behind strict networks cannot connect, because there is no relay server for the traffic itself (TURN). Every online test so far ran on one machine.

Entry 6 · 16 October 2026 · the five-runner test

Five browsers play one match, and an agent watches

tests/online/runners.py starts five separate Chromium processes, each recorded on video. One hosts; four join by the room link; each picks a hero, hands it to the computer and also clicks and presses keys, so its own orders travel the network. The five human seats and five computer seats make a 5v5. Midway one browser is closed without warning and reopened on its link. A separate agent read the log every 45 seconds while it ran and wrote notes/ONLINE-RUN.md.

Between the runs: the browsers no longer chase each other's one-tick lead; silence is timed by the clock, not by ticks that have stopped; the returning player's log goes in pieces; a transport error no longer replaces a running match's status. After the second run, the analyst found that other joiners still marked the returning seat as away; the host now tells everyone.

Entry 7 · 16 October 2026 · published

Live, and what to try first

The game is at sgilson7.github.io/pots; the code at github.com/sgilson7/pots. The full check passed before publishing, and the browser check passed again on the live address in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit. Two browsers met and played online on the live address, and a dropped one came back, three times in three.

Try by hand

Three things worth ten minutes

  1. Mission 1

    Walk to the middle lane and land last hits as creeps drop low. The goal line counts them.

  2. A custom 5v5

    Buy at the base shop (B), learn abilities with the plus under each, and push a tower with your allies.

  3. Online on two networks

    Host on one computer, send the link to another on a different network, both pick, start; close one browser mid-match and reopen its link.
