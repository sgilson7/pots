How Builder carries it

Where each part lives

LayerFor this game
Core (Rust)The whole match, at a fixed tick in integer maths with a seeded random source: movement and pathing around trees, attacks and the attack-type table, armor, abilities, items and recipes, creep waves, neutral camps, the boss, runes, gold and experience, death and respawn, vision and fog, and every computer player's behavior tree. It also builds the camera, the 3D scene list and the HUD description. Same seed and same inputs give the same match on every machine, which is what rollback needs.
Shim (wasm)Passes orders in and the scene out. Decides nothing.

OnlineBrowsers connect to each other directly and exchange orders, tick by tick. Each browser runs the match itself, guesses what late orders were, and rolls back and replays when the real order arrives. A rejoining browser receives the match state and the orders since.

Decided without asking

Choices the brief left open

Names

Every hero, item, creep, building and the boss has an original name and look. Abilities keep their behaviour and numbers. The two sides are the Sunward and the Moonward, each guarding a Sphere. You read the names before they are final.

The DotA version

The game follows the 6.6x versions (2009 to 2011), the era most sources describe and most players remember. Where sources disagree, the review says which version a number comes from.

Online without a server

GitHub Pages serves files only, so browsers find each other through a public signaling service and then talk directly. That service is the one request to another site, and it loads only when a player opens online play.

Computer players online

Empty seats in an online room are filled by computer players. They run inside the match itself, so every browser computes the same choices.

Kept exactly

Entry 5 · 16 October 2026 · online mode

Ten browsers, rollback, and coming back

A host opens a room and shares its code or link; up to nine others join. Empty seats are played by the computer. Browsers meet through public Nostr relays and then talk to each other directly.

How it works

Rollback over orders

Every browser runs the match

The rules are deterministic: the same seed and orders give the same match bit for bit. Only orders cross the network, never the world.

Guess, then correct

A browser's own orders act two ticks later. Another seat's late order is guessed as "nothing new", which is right on most ticks; when it arrives, the world is restored to before that tick and run again.

Coming back

The address keeps a seat token. When a player drops, the computer takes the seat in every browser. On return the host sends every order so far; the browser replays the match at speed and takes the seat back.

What it cannot do yet.

If the host's browser leaves, the match ends. Two players both behind strict networks cannot connect, because there is no relay server for the traffic itself (TURN). Every online test so far ran on one machine.

Entry 6 · 16 October 2026 · the five-runner test

Five browsers play one match, and an agent watches

tests/online/runners.py starts five separate Chromium processes, each recorded on video. One hosts; four join by the room link; each picks a hero, hands it to the computer and also clicks and presses keys, so its own orders travel the network. The five human seats and five computer seats make a 5v5. Midway one browser is closed without warning and reopened on its link. A separate agent read the log every 45 seconds while it ran and wrote notes/ONLINE-RUN.md.

Why the game looks blocky now

The cubes are only one of three causes. The light is the second: the shader lights every surface in three hard bands with no shadow, so even a sphere reads as facets. The proportions are the third: small heads and thin limbs read as soldiers, not toys.

The new look

Eight rules

  1. Nothing alive has a corner

    Spheres, capsules, cylinders, cones and torus arcs only. Buildings are drums, domes, cones and rounded stone.

  2. Chibi proportions

    A hero's head is about 40 % of its height, the torso 30 %, the legs 20 %, with oversized hands and feet. Creeps are smaller and simpler; buildings far bigger.

  3. Faces carry the style

    Two dot eyes set low and wide, a catch-light in each, blush beneath. Glowing eyes mark the sinister ones.

  4. Each hero keeps its round-1 cue

    The blade, the hood and staff, the flame hair, the bow, the belly and hook, the horns and totem, the mask, the claw staff, the long rifle, the cloud crown.

  5. One thin warm outline

    A plum outline about 1.5 % of a hero's height round every solid part, as on Chumbles. Glows have none.

  6. Soft light

    A warm key with soft shadows, a sky-blue fill, a warm bounce from the ground. Never hard bands.

  7. Team colour on every unit

    Sunward gold and cream, Moonward violet and slate, and a soft ring in the side's colour under every unit. Colour is never the only cue.

  8. Every ability in four beats

    A cast pose; a flight or a warning on the ground; an impact with a flash; then a mark on whoever is affected for as long as it lasts. The marks are the same across the game: stars for a stun, frost and a blue tint for a slow, a crossed bubble for a silence, a coloured ring for a buff.

Part 3 · the recommendation

A faceted ground

The biggest change is to draw the ground as one faceted surface instead of a block per cell. The ground is the largest thing on screen, and its staircase edges are the plainest sign of an unfinished game. The change fixes gaps 1 and 5 at once and makes room for 4. It also finishes the look you chose for the map in T19: the trees, towers and rocks are already faceted with no outline, and the ground is the one map piece still drawn as blocks. The characters keep their look.

How the prototype draws it

  1. Corners every half cell (32 map units). Each corner takes its height and colour from the cells around it, so the edge between two kinds of ground is a slope and a gradient.
  2. Height blends over a narrower span than colour, so a cliff stays a short, steep face while its colours blend.
  3. Each corner gets a small seeded nudge in height and shade, the same in every browser, so the facets catch the light unevenly, as the faceted trees and towers do.
  4. Each triangle is lit by its own slope and turns to rock where it is steeper than about 35°.
  5. The forest floor is darker under standing trees.
  6. The water is one flat sheet over a sunken riverbed. Where the banks slope down into it, the shoreline follows the slope.

  • Hit as many heroes as possible, with Ice Vortex first (its 1500 range makes that easy).
  • "As long as the carry is hit with the DoT, that is usually enough, because now they can't life steal, or be healed."
  • A global finisher, timed by watching the tracer on the minimap.

Not settled by any source, and what the game uses

  • The hail's speed: at least 750 a second, landing within about 2 s (guides); built as min(distance ÷ 750, 2 s).
  • An unreleased tracer: here it waits at the map's edge. If Shiver dies, it is released (as in the Dota 2 port).
  • Frostbite's tick: here smooth, as every damage over time in the game is.
  • Cold Feet's hits come every 0.85 s here, against the source's 0.8, 1.6, 2.5 and 3.4 s.
  • Turn rate (0.6, the port's) and acquisition range (800).
  • Ice Vortex's vision and the tracer's vision are not built: sight in the game comes from units.
Part 2

Its look

Not a skeletal frost spirit, and nothing borrowed from Blizzard or Valve. It is round and faceted like the other twelve, with an outline that reads from the raised camera: the owl's head and ear tufts, two wide wings, and no legs. In the game it floats, its body rising and falling every two seconds and its wings beating a little.

Frozen Ground

What you see. Frost spreads on the ground where the enemy stood, a thin cold line runs to its feet, and ice spikes grow round the spot as the seconds pass. A faint ring shows how far it must go to break free.

How you can tell. If the enemy is still inside the ring after 4 seconds, it is frozen in a block of ice. Walking out of the ring breaks the ice and stops the hits.

Ice grips an enemy where it stands. It takes 37.5 / 50 / 62.5 / 75 magic damage 4 times, and if it is still within 740 of that spot after 4 seconds, it is stunned for 1.25 / 2 / 2.75 / 3.5 seconds. Mana: 150 · Cooldown: 15 / 13 / 11 / 9 s

Snow Whirl

What you see. A pale disc with three arms of snow turning inward. Enemies in it get the frost ring of a slow.

How you can tell. The vortex itself is the area: inside it, spells hurt more. It lasts 12 seconds and does not stack with a second one.

A whirl of snow spins 275 round a point for 12 seconds. Enemies in it are slowed by 18% / 22% / 26% / 30% and lose 10% / 15% / 20% / 25% magic resistance. Mana: 80 / 90 / 100 / 110 · Cooldown: 6 s

Frost Talons

What you see. A frost ring opens round the point, and each allied hero in it gets ice shards circling its chest, one for each charge.

How you can tell. A shard goes with each attack on an enemy hero, and a frost flash marks the extra hit. Attacks on creeps keep the shards.

Allied heroes within 450 of the point, Shiver too if it stands there, deal 40 / 50 / 60 / 70 more magic damage with each of their next 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 attacks on enemy heroes. Their attacks are 15% slower until the charges are spent or 40 seconds pass. Mana: 140 · Cooldown: 50 / 46 / 42 / 38 s

Hailstar

Ultimate

What you see. A small star of hail flies off across the map. When it is released, a chunk of ice falls from Shiver to the star, a ring on the ground showing where it will burst, then bursts in a ring of ice spikes.

How you can tell. The longer the star flew, the wider the ring. Frostbitten enemies have a snowflake over their heads and frost at their feet. Enemies never see the star.

Sends a star of hail toward a point anywhere on the map. Press again to burst it where it flies: the longer it flew, the wider the burst, from 225 to 1000. Enemies hit take 250 / 350 / 450 magic damage and 12.5 / 20 / 32 a second for 8 / 9 / 10 seconds, cannot heal, and die below 10% / 11% / 12% health. Mana: 100 / 125 / 150 · Cooldown: 45 s

Hailfall

Ultimate

What you see. The star stops where it is and the hail falls on it.

How you can tell. Once the hail is falling, the button is Hailstar again, cooling down from the first cast.

Bursts the Hailstar where it is now. The hail falls from Shiver to that spot.
